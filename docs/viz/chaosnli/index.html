Panel 02 · Stability Analysis

49% of Items Have Ambiguous Neighbors

Storage-Index Boundary Tie Instability

Standard AI methods silently choose which "neighbors" an item has when there are tied distances—and that choice changes if you reorder your dataset. Our fractional method is immune: its score is always exactly 1.000, regardless of data ordering.

When multiple candidates share the exact $k$-th boundary distance, index-resolved methods produce unstable graphs ($\bar{Q}_{\text{hard}} = 0.955 \pm 0.0015$ at $k=10$). The fractional soft statistic $Q_{NX}^{\text{soft}}$ is provably invariant: $Q_{\text{soft}} = 1.000 \pm 0.000$.

Panel 04 · Phase Analysis

ChaosNLI Has a Specific Disagreement Fingerprint

Dirichlet Concentration Phase Diagram ($C=3$, Boundary Tie Rate)

We modeled disagreement patterns across many simulated conditions to find where ChaosNLI sits. At 49.1% of items having ambiguous neighbors, the data falls between two theoretical regimes — not a perfect match for either, but precisely placed.

Boundary tie percentage $\mathbb{P}[\text{tie at boundary}]$ across Dirichlet concentration $\alpha$ and annotation depth $n$ for $C=3$ labels. Empirical ChaosNLI rate (49.1% at $n=100$) lies between $\alpha=0.1$ (80.2%) and $\alpha=0.5$ (35.7%) — compatible with both, uniquely identified by neither.

Panel 07 · Robustness

1,000 Random Checks — All Confirmed the Gap

Stratified Focal-Item Bootstrap: $\Delta_{m,b} > 0$ for all 1,000 replicates

We ran 1,000 different random sub-samples of the data—each time checking whether AI models still scored below the human baseline. Every single one confirmed the gap. No random rearrangement made the AI models look competitive.

In all 1,000 stratified focal-item bootstrap replicates (SNLI + MNLI strata), $\Delta_{m,b} = H_b - M_{m,b} > 0$ for every evaluated model. Minimum 95% CI lower bound: $+0.054$ (BART-Large). The bootstrap resamples items within the fixed ChaosNLI candidate population.

Appendix · Study 2A
Exploratory: Does annotator identity matter? VariErr External Validation (Study 2A) — Exploratory / Inconclusive

We also explored whether individual annotators have consistent personal styles across different items. Out of 52 annotator groups we found, the within-group SD was 7.8% lower than expected by chance—a suggestive pattern, but not statistically conclusive (p = 0.20 across 500,000 permutations). This is an interesting direction for future research, not a confirmed finding. Matched 500 items across 52 multi-item annotator profiles. Within-profile SD = 0.106 vs. null mean = 0.115 (7.8% reduction). Permutation p-value = 0.2045 (500,000 permutations). Consistent with either small signal or sampling limitations from the 52-profile restriction. Appropriately inconclusive.
